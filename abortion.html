Human Life and Abortion

When does human life begin? And what does justice require once it has begun?

1. The Central Question

The abortion debate is often framed in terms of women's rights, autonomy, or healthcare. But logically, the core ethical question is much simpler:

What is the unborn?

If the unborn child is merely a clump of cells or part of a woman's body, abortion is morally permissible. But if the unborn is a distinct human life, then abortion intentionally ends an innocent human life.

The entire debate therefore hinges on the status of the unborn.

2. What Does Science Say?

Modern embryology is clear that a new human organism begins at fertilisation. At conception a genetically unique human organism is formed with its own DNA and developmental trajectory.

From that point onward the embryo is not merely potential life — it is a living human organism in the earliest stage of development.

The question therefore shifts from biology to ethics: Should developing humans have moral protection?

3. Human Equality and Moral Value

Historically, societies have denied personhood to certain groups — slaves, ethnic minorities, or the disabled. These exclusions were later recognised as grave moral errors.

The pro-life position argues that moral value should not depend on:

  • Size
  • Level of development
  • Environment (inside or outside the womb)
  • Degree of dependence

None of these characteristics determine whether someone is human. They simply describe stages of development.

If all humans are equal, then the smallest and most vulnerable humans should also be protected.

4. The Autonomy Argument

Many argue that abortion should remain legal because women have a right to bodily autonomy.

Bodily autonomy is an important moral principle. However, autonomy is never absolute.

For example, autonomy does not allow someone to harm another innocent human being. Society routinely restricts personal freedom where another person's life is at stake.

If the unborn child is a human being, then abortion is not simply a private medical decision. It becomes a question of justice between two human lives.

5. Taking the Logic of “Choice” to its End

If abortion is justified purely on the basis of personal choice, then logically the reason for the abortion should not matter.

This leads to troubling implications.

One example is sex-selective abortion — terminating a pregnancy solely because the child is female.

Globally this practice has led to millions of missing girls in countries with strong cultural son preference.

Evidence suggests that similar patterns have appeared even in Western countries. Research examining over two million births in Australia found skewed male-to-female birth ratios among certain migrant populations, which researchers believe provides indirect evidence of prenatal sex selection followed by abortion of female fetuses.

This pattern was particularly pronounced among families who already had two daughters, where birth ratios reached roughly 133 boys for every 100 girls in some groups.

Such findings suggest that when fetal sex can be determined early through modern testing, some pregnancies are terminated specifically because the unborn child is female.

This creates a difficult moral tension for a purely pro-choice framework:

  • If abortion is justified purely by choice, then sex-selective abortion must also be allowed.
  • If sex-selective abortion is morally wrong, then abortion cannot be justified by choice alone.

The logic of choice eventually forces us back to the original question: What is the unborn?

6. The Biblical Perspective

The Bible consistently affirms the value of human life before birth.

Psalm 139:13–16 describes God forming a person in the womb:

"For you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother's womb."

Similarly, the prophet Jeremiah writes:

"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you." (Jeremiah 1:5)

The biblical worldview treats unborn life not as disposable biological material, but as human life known and valued by God.

7. A Culture of Compassion

Christians should approach this topic with humility and compassion. Many women face extremely difficult circumstances during pregnancy.

A pro-life ethic does not merely oppose abortion. It should also support:

  • practical care for mothers
  • adoption
  • family support
  • social policies that make raising children easier

The goal is not condemnation but a culture that protects both mother and child.
