Human Life and Abortion

The abortion debate is often framed around rights, autonomy, and personal freedom. But beneath these discussions lies a deeper question: what is the unborn?

Anchor posture

“Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves, for the rights of all who are destitute.” (Proverbs 31:8)

1) The central question

Most abortion debates avoid the core issue. If the unborn child is not human, abortion raises little moral concern. But if the unborn is a developing human life, abortion becomes the deliberate ending of an innocent human being.

The entire debate therefore hinges on one question: What is the unborn?

2) What science says

Modern embryology indicates that a new human organism begins at fertilisation. At that moment a genetically distinct human organism forms, with its own DNA and developmental trajectory.

From fertilisation onward the embryo grows continuously through different stages of human development: embryo, fetus, infant, child, and adult.

The biological question is largely settled. The ethical question remains: should developing humans have moral protection?

3) Human equality

  • Human beings differ in size.
  • Human beings differ in development.
  • Human beings differ in dependency.
  • Human beings differ in location.

But none of these characteristics determine whether someone is human. They simply describe stages of development.

Historically, societies have denied personhood to certain groups before recognising this as a grave moral error. The pro-life argument claims that unborn humans should not be excluded from the human community.

4) The autonomy argument

Many argue abortion should remain legal because women have a right to bodily autonomy. Autonomy is an important principle, but it is not unlimited.

Society routinely limits personal freedom when another human life is at risk. For example, laws against assault or neglect restrict what individuals can do to others.

If the unborn child is a human being, then abortion is not simply a private medical decision but a question of justice between two human lives.

5) Following the logic of “choice”

If abortion is justified purely on the basis of personal choice, then logically the reason for the abortion should not matter.

However, this raises difficult questions when abortion is used for sex-selection — terminating a pregnancy because the child is female.

Research analysing Australian birth records has identified male-skewed sex ratios among certain migrant communities, particularly when families already have daughters. Some researchers interpret this as evidence of prenatal sex selection.

For example, studies examining millions of Australian births have found ratios as high as roughly 133 boys for every 100 girls in specific circumstances.

This creates a tension for a purely pro-choice framework:

  • If abortion is justified purely by choice, sex-selective abortion must also be permitted.
  • If sex-selective abortion is morally wrong, then choice alone cannot justify abortion.

The logic eventually returns us to the deeper question: What is the unborn?

6) Biblical perspective

  • Psalm 139:13-16 — God forms human life in the womb.
  • Jeremiah 1:5 — God knows a person before birth.
  • Luke 1:41-44 — John the Baptist responds to Jesus while both are still in the womb.

The biblical worldview consistently treats unborn life as meaningful and known by God.

7) Speak with compassion

This topic involves deeply personal circumstances. Many women face fear, pressure, and difficult decisions during pregnancy.

Christians should therefore approach the subject with humility and compassion, offering support for both mothers and children.

A pro-life ethic includes practical care:

  • support for mothers
  • adoption
  • community care
  • practical help during pregnancy
