Common Questions

These are some of the most common objections and questions Christians face today. The goal is not to “win arguments” but to offer clear, calm, and thoughtful responses.

Anchor posture

“Let your conversation be always full of grace, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how to answer everyone.” (Colossians 4:6)

1) Why does God allow suffering?

The question

If God is good and powerful, why does suffering exist?

A calm response

Christianity teaches that God created a good world, but human rebellion introduced brokenness. God allows freedom, which makes love possible, but also allows for real harm.

Suffering can produce character and growth. God does not remain distant; in Jesus, He enters our suffering.

Natural disasters, disease, and human injustice are part of a fallen world. Compassion and action are our call while trusting God's overarching wisdom.

2) Isn’t Christianity just one religion among many?

The question

Why should anyone believe Christianity instead of other religions?

A calm response

Different religions make different and often contradictory claims. They cannot all be true in the same way.

Christianity centers on historical claims about Jesus, particularly His death and resurrection. The question is not simply preference, but truth.

3) Has science disproved God?

The question

Doesn’t science explain everything without God?

A calm response

Science explains how the natural world works, but it does not answer why the universe exists at all, or why it is intelligible.

The existence of laws of nature, rationality, and order fits well within a theistic worldview.

4) Isn’t the Bible unreliable?

The question

The Bible has been copied and translated many times. How can we trust it?

A calm response

While minor variations exist in manuscripts, the core message of the Bible is well preserved.

Compared to other ancient documents, the New Testament is extremely well attested historically.

5) Isn’t Christianity oppressive or harmful?

The question

Hasn’t Christianity caused harm throughout history?

A calm response

Christians, like all people, have sometimes acted wrongly. But the truth of Christianity is not determined by its worst representatives.

The teachings of Jesus consistently emphasise love, humility, and sacrifice.

6) How do we respond to moral relativism?

The question

If truth is personal, why should anyone follow a moral standard?

A calm response

Relativism avoids hard questions about truth. Christianity teaches that objective moral standards exist because God is the ultimate moral authority.

Understanding right and wrong as grounded in God allows consistent moral reasoning and a framework for justice and compassion.

7) Why is prayer necessary if God already knows everything?

The question

If God is omniscient, why pray at all?

A calm response

Prayer is not to inform God, but to align our hearts with His will and participate in His work.

It develops trust, dependence, and humility, while inviting God’s power to work in real-life circumstances.

8) Can Christianity coexist with secular humanism?

The question

If we all aim for compassion and fairness, why does it matter if someone is non-religious?

A calm response

Secular humanism can produce moral behavior, but without an ultimate foundation, values become subjective and inconsistent.

Christianity offers a coherent worldview where morality, purpose, and hope are anchored in God, not merely human opinion.

9) What about evil and injustice in the world?

The question

How can a good God allow widespread evil and systemic injustice?

A calm response

God allows freedom and responsibility, which unfortunately lets evil exist. Yet He promises ultimate justice and redemption.

Christians are called to respond with compassion and action, reflecting God’s character in a broken world.
