General Resources

A curated collection of books, podcasts, and materials for thoughtful Christians — and for those genuinely exploring the truth claims of Christianity. Start where you feel comfortable and go deeper at your own pace.

General Reading (For Believers)

Tim Keller – The Reason for God

A clear and thoughtful response to common objections to Christianity. Accessible yet intellectually serious.

C.S. Lewis – Mere Christianity

A classic articulation of core Christian belief, written with clarity and reason.

William Lane Craig – Reasonable Faith

A deeper dive into philosophical and historical arguments for Christianity.

Gary Habermas & Michael Licona – The Case for the Resurrection of Jesus

Focused exploration of the historical evidence surrounding the resurrection.

Podcasts & Ongoing Learning

I Don’t Have Enough Faith to Be an Atheist

Explores arguments for God’s existence, historical reliability of Scripture, and contemporary cultural challenges.

Undeceptions

Thoughtful discussions on faith, ethics, history, and cultural questions — particularly helpful in an Australian context.

Cold-Case Christianity

Examines Christianity through investigative reasoning, focusing on the reliability of the Gospel accounts.

For Skeptics & Honest Questioners

Lee Strobel – The Case for Christ

Written from the perspective of a former skeptic investigating the historical claims about Jesus.

John Lennox – Can Science Explain Everything?

A short, accessible examination of the relationship between science and belief in God.

Michael Green – Jesus for Skeptics

A very short and easy to read guide addressing some key questions about Christianity.

Ben Shaw – 7 Reasons to Reconsider Christianity

Perfect for skeptics, identifying 7 reasons why Christianity is worth considering/re-considering.

Rebecca Mclaughlin – Confronting Christianity

Using research, lived experience, and biblical analysis, Confronting Christianity responds to twelve major objections to Christian belief.

The Gospel of John (New Testament)

For those exploring firsthand — start with one of the primary sources. Read it slowly and consider what it claims about Jesus.

Resources are curated for clarity, credibility, and thoughtful engagement — not outrage.
