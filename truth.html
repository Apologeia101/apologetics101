The Truth of Christianity

Christianity is not merely a feeling, a private preference, or a tribal identity. It makes public claims about reality—especially about Jesus. This guide offers a calm, structured way to think about those claims.

Anchor posture

“In your hearts honour Christ the Lord as holy, always being prepared to make a defence (apologia)… yet do it with gentleness and respect.” (1 Peter 3:15)

1) The common claims

  • “The Bible has been corrupted.”
  • “Jesus is a myth.”
  • “All religions are basically the same.”
  • “Science has disproved God.”
  • “Christianity is harmful/oppressive.”

2) Why these claims feel persuasive

  • Miracles challenge modern assumptions about what can happen.
  • Institutional failures make people suspicious of all religious authority.
  • Relativism sounds humble (“who am I to say?”), but it avoids truth questions.
  • People confuse “some Christians were wrong” with “Christ is false.”

3) Hidden assumptions to notice

  • Naturalism: only matter exists, so miracles can’t happen.
  • Relativism: truth is personal, not objective.
  • Moralism: “a good person wouldn’t believe hard doctrines.”
  • Guilt by association: abuse by some leaders disproves Christianity.

4) Biblical foundations

  • 1 Peter 3:15 — be prepared to give a reasoned defence with gentleness.
  • Luke 1:1–4 — Christianity presents itself as careful history, grounded in testimony.
  • 2 Peter 1:16 — not “cleverly devised myths.”
  • 1 Corinthians 15:14–19 — the resurrection is central; if false, faith collapses.
  • John 14:6 — Jesus makes an exclusive truth claim.

5) A reasoned defence

A. Is Jesus a myth?

The serious question isn’t “Was Jesus perfect?” but “Did he exist?” The mainstream historical view is that Jesus of Nazareth existed and was crucified. The debate is over what he claimed and whether he rose from the dead.

B. Has the Bible been corrupted?

The Bible was copied by hand for centuries, so minor variations exist. But the message is remarkably well-attested across many manuscripts. Most variants are spelling/word-order differences and do not change core doctrine.

C. Did the resurrection happen?

Christianity stands or falls on an event in history. The earliest Christian message centres on the resurrection, and the New Testament treats it as public fact—not private symbolism. Alternative explanations (legend, hallucination, conspiracy) each face serious difficulties when taken seriously.

D. Has science disproved God?

Science studies the natural world. God is not a “thing inside the universe” to be tested like a chemical. The deeper question is: why is there a universe at all, and why is it intelligible? Christianity historically affirms both rational order and moral meaning.

6) Calm responses you can actually use

“All religions are the same.”

They make different and sometimes contradictory claims. They can’t all be true in the same way.

“The Bible has been corrupted.”

What specific teaching do you think changed? Most textual differences are minor and well-documented.

“Science disproves God.”

Science explains processes inside the universe. God is a claim about ultimate reality—why anything exists at all.

“Christians have done terrible things.”

Yes—and that’s sin. But the truth of Jesus isn’t decided by the failures of those who claim his name.

7) Speak with gentleness

Apologetics is not permission to be harsh. Aim for clarity, not cruelty. Ask good questions, listen carefully, and remember: a calm tone often communicates confidence more than a clever line.
